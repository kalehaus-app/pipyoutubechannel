The only variable that changed is **what the video is about**.

### The rule

Every winner is a **concrete physical activity with visible mess and a face
the toddler pulls**:

- Bath → water everywhere
- Candy → the object, and a refusal
- Brush teeth → foam
- Blow nose → tissue, snot
- Medicine → the spoon, the yuck face
- Dentist → mouth open, the chair

Every loser is **abstract or tidy**:

- Feelings (bye-bye, bravery, friendship, imagination) — nothing to look at
- Tidy chores (make bed, cut nails, wash off sand) — no mess, no resistance

So the test for a new concept is not "is this a healthy habit". It is:

> **Can a two-year-old see the mess, and does the kid pull a face about it?**

If the answer to either half is no, the topic does not work on this channel
regardless of how well it is executed.

### Runtime

Every winner is **29–31 seconds**. Below that, results collapse (Make Your Bed
20s, Wash Off the Sand 23s, Adventure Day 23s). Above it too — Haircut is a
strong resistance topic and ran 50s for 143 views; Shoes On ran 36s for 424.

**Hold new Shorts to 28–31 seconds.** Treat anything outside that as a defect.

## Story content does not work here

| Video | Views |
|---|---:|
| Pip & Teddy — Best Friends | 164 |
| Pip & Teddy's Adventure Day | 138 |
| Pip Goes Down the Big Water Slide | 95 |
| Pip's Water Park Fun Day (5 min) | 4 |

Four attempts, four flat results, across both formats. Meanwhile the
habit-format long-form did 12,749. This corrects an earlier read in
`docs/MONETIZATION.md`: the split is **not** long-form vs Shorts. It is
**habit content vs story content**. Habit content wins in both formats.

Narrative adventures are a nice thing to make. They are not what this audience
clicks, and they should not consume generation credits while monetization is
the goal.

## Long-form deserves a second look

The Robosquad-style long-form produced **152.7 watch hours from one upload** —
19% of the channel's entire 30-day watch time, from a single video.

This matters because YPP's alternative gate is 3,000 public watch hours, and
**Shorts watch time does not count toward it**. At ~150 hours per long-form
upload, roughly 20 such videos clears the gate. That is a far shorter path than
3,000,000 Shorts views.

Two things to check in Studio before betting on it:

1. **Re-open the Earn tab.** It read *4 qualified watch hours / 365 days* as of
   Aug 20, which cannot be squared with 152.7 hours from one video in the same
   month. Either the figure was stale or something is disqualifying that watch
   time. Everything below depends on which.
2. **Open Traffic Sources for that video.** It shows 12,749 views against only
   200 thumbnail impressions, so those views did not come from thumbnails. Where
   they did come from tells you whether the result is repeatable.

Also worth noting: its title is keyword-first —
`Brush Teeth Together | No Cavity Day | Funny Kids Songs & Nursery Rhymes | Robosquad`
— phrases parents actually search. Compare
`Pip's Water Park Fun Day 💦🐻 | Fun Story for Toddlers | Pip | Shows for Little Ones`,
which leads with a proper noun nobody searches for. Lead long-form titles with
the activity and the search phrase, never with the character name.

## Cadence is the other half

Daily views: **first 7 days averaged 15,491/day. Last 7 days averaged 761.**
A 95% collapse.

Publish dates tell the story — Aug 4, 5, 6, 7, 10, 11, 12, 13, then **nothing
until Aug 26**. The channel's four best videos all launched inside one 8-day
run of daily uploads, and views fell off a cliff the moment uploading stopped.

Consistency is not a nice-to-have here. It is visible in the data as the thing
that was working.

## Honest math on 3,000,000 Shorts views

| Basis | 90-day projection |
|---|---:|
| Last 7 days (761/day) | 68,000 |
| Full 30 days (6,035/day) | 543,000 |
| **Required** | **3,000,000** |

Even at the good 30-day average, that is **5.5x short** — and that average is
propped up by one 94K outlier. Reaching 3M in 90 days does not mean uploading
more consistently; it means landing several more Bath-Time-scale hits *and*
uploading consistently.

That is achievable but it is not a schedule you can promise. Which is exactly
why the watch-hours route is worth re-checking first — it needs ~20 good
long-form uploads, not several viral lottery wins.

### Held back

**Sneeze in Your Elbow 🤧** — sound topic (Blow Your Nose did 11,906) but too
close to it to run in the same batch. Good slot 7.

**No More Screen Time 📱** — strong resistance topic, but it risks reading as
preachy to parents rather than fun for kids. Test only after the six above.

Do **not** remake: Comb Your Hair, Cut Your Nails, Make Your Bed, Set the Table,
Shoes On, Buckle Up, Car Seat, Sun Hat. All tested, all under 450 views. The
topics are the problem, not the executions.
